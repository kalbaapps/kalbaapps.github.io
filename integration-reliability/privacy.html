Privacy Policy

Integration Reliability (Android app) · Effective 5 October 2026

In short: the app has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and the developer does not receive or store any of your data. The app only talks to the server that you enter.

What the app is

Integration Reliability is a companion app for the Integration Reliability server. You run (or are given access to) a server that checks your own systems. The app shows you the results from that server and lets you add checks and receive alerts.

What is stored on your phone

  • The server address you typed.
  • Your sign-in token for that server, if you signed in with a password.
  • Whether phone alerts are switched on for this phone.

This stays on your phone. Choosing Change server / sign out in Settings removes it. Uninstalling the app also removes it.

What the app sends, and to whom

  • To your server: requests to read check results and to add or remove checks. Which server receives them is your choice. The operator of that server (often you) is responsible for the data on it. The developer of this app has no access to it.
  • Phone alerts (optional): if you switch them on, the app asks Android for notification permission and obtains an anonymous push address from Expo's push service (which uses Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging). The app sends that push address and the phone model name (for example "Pixel 8") to your server, so that your server can notify your phone. Your server then asks Expo's push service to deliver each alert. Alerts contain the name of the check and a short error description from your server.
  • Demo mode: uses made-up sample data on the phone. Nothing is sent anywhere.

What we do not do

  • No accounts are created, and the developer collects no personal information.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and no sale or sharing of data.
  • The app does not read your contacts, location, photos, files, microphone or camera.

Third-party services involved in alerts

Expo (expo.dev/privacy) and Google Firebase Cloud Messaging (policies.google.com/privacy) deliver push notifications. They process the anonymous push address and the alert text in order to deliver it. They act under their own privacy policies.

Your choices

  • Turn off phone alerts any time in Settings (this removes the push address from your server).
  • Sign out or change server in Settings to clear everything the app stored.
  • Revoke notification permission in your phone's system settings.

Security

The store version of the app only connects to servers over HTTPS (encrypted in transit). Protect your server with a strong password.

Children

The app is a business and engineering tool and is not directed at children.
